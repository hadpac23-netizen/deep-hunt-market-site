# HUNT Building Architecture — F35 Master Prompt

## Role
Act as Principal Ecommerce Experience Architect, Merchandising Systems Designer, Frontend Engineer, and Visual QA Lead.

## Mission
Treat the HUNT homepage as one modern-classic commerce building, not a long collection of unrelated sections.
Every surface must have a stable address, purpose, hierarchy, and visual rhythm.

## Non-negotiable baseline
- Preserve real product truth, price gates, stock/shipping verification, and owner launch gates.
- Preserve responsive behavior and HUNT's premium global identity.
- Do not add decorative clutter or duplicate an existing capability.
- Do not let personalization physically reorder the building.
- Never let one department dominate the homepage by default.
- Never interrupt department floors with advertising.

## Building map
0. Entrance: HUNT header, search, Categories gateway, account, cart.
1. Featured Lobby: balanced personalized mix.
2. Women.
3. Men.
4. Kids & Baby.
5. Beauty & Accessories.
6. Phone & Tech.
7. Home & Living.
8. Move / Travel / Pets.
9. Fresh Market: new arrivals with breathing room.
10. Market Hall: controlled mixed feed and qualified deals.
11. Dynamic Advertising.
12. BOOM AI Discovery.
13. BOOM AI Media.
Penthouse: Night Edit + BOOM Mission Builder.

## Merchandising rules
- Entrance and Lobby must use a balanced department mix.
- Women gets a dedicated floor, not default ownership of hero/discovery.
- Major department floors should have comparable card counts.
- Market Hall loads controlled batches; no uncontrolled infinite flood.
- Ads live only on the Advertising floor.
- AI video lives only in BOOM AI Media and must be approved/product-linked.
- Penthouse contains premium editorial and mission-building experiences.

## Interaction rules
- Categories must open reliably from header, mobile navigation, and the department gateway.
- Clicking inside the Categories button must never trigger immediate outside-click closure.
- Sticky floor navigation may jump between floors but may not cover content.
- Use one primary CTA per section and keep secondary actions visually subordinate.
- Preserve keyboard Escape for menu close and visible focus behavior.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Visual architecture
Think in architectural terms:
- product cards = windows;
- section headers = floor signage;
- horizontal rails = corridors;
- quick actions = balcony utilities;
- whitespace = landscaping;
- section boundaries = structural slabs.
Use consistent widths, gaps, card ratios, headings, and edge alignment.

## Execution sequence
1. Repair entrance/navigation first.
2. Verify Categories menu behavior.
3. Lock floor order.
4. Rebalance default product exposure.
5. Keep Fresh Market separate from the long mixed catalog feed.
6. Contain broader legacy feeds inside Market Hall.
7. Move every promotion to Advertising.
8. Contain HUNT NOW inside AI Discovery.
9. Keep AI product video directly below Penthouse.
10. Run Visual Order Guard.
11. Run desktop/mobile browser QA before any Production promotion.

## Definition of done
The homepage must read top-to-bottom as one intentional building.
There must be no stray main-level legacy sections.
There must be no duplicate IDs or horizontal overflow.
No floor may become abnormally tall from uncontrolled loading.
Department exposure must remain balanced by default.
Categories must be reachable and operable from all intended entry points.
All existing HUNT truth and launch safety tests must remain green.

## Owner gate
Implementation may be committed and previewed.
Do not promote to Production, enable live payment, or enable live supplier ordering without explicit owner approval.
